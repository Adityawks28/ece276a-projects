\section{Introduction}

Indoors there is no GPS, and odometry drifts: on dataset 20 it ends 2.77\,m
from the start after a 95.9\,m drive. SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and
Mapping) estimates the path and the map together, so that seeing the same
wall twice corrects the path.

The system has four stages for a differential-drive robot with encoders, an
IMU, a 2-D LiDAR and a Kinect~\cite{atanasov} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline}):
(1)~encoder and IMU odometry, (2)~ICP scan matching, (3)~pose graph
optimization with loop closures in GTSAM~\cite{gtsam}, and (4)~occupancy grid
and floor texture mapping with the optimized poses. The final path ends
0.12\,m (dataset 20) and 0.22\,m (dataset 21) from the start, compared to 2.7
to 3.3\,m for odometry or ICP alone.

\section{Problem Formulation}

\emph{Pose.} $\x=(x,y,\theta)$ with $\theta\in[-\pi,\pi)$, and matrix form
\begin{equation}
T(\x)=\begin{bmatrix}R(\theta) & \tb\\ \mathbf{0}^\top & 1\end{bmatrix}
=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta & -\sin\theta & x\\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta & y\\ 0&0&1\end{bmatrix},
\end{equation}
which maps a body point to the world: $\p_W=R\p_B+\tb$. The pose of $j$
relative to $i$ is $T_i^{-1}T_j$. For scan $k$, $T_k=T(\x_k)$ has rotation
$R_k=R(\theta_k)$ and translation $\tb_k$. The unknowns are the path
$\{\x_0,\dots,\x_{K-1}\}$ (one pose for each of the $K$ LiDAR scans), the occupancy map $m$ and
the floor color map $c$.

\emph{Motion model.} With speed $v_t$, yaw rate $\omega_t$, time step $\tau_t$:
\begin{equation}
\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\omega_t\tau_t,\quad
\begin{bmatrix}x_{t+1}\\y_{t+1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x_t\\y_t\end{bmatrix}+v_t\tau_t\begin{bmatrix}\cos\theta_{t+1}\\\sin\theta_{t+1}\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:motion}
\end{equation}

\emph{Registration.} For point sets $\{\p_k\}$, $\{\q_k\}$ with known pairs,
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
(R^*,\tb^*)=\arg\min_{R,\,\tb}\ \sum_{k=1}^{N} \lVert \q_k-(R\p_k+\tb)\rVert^2\\
\text{subject to}\quad R^\top R=I,\ \det R=1.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:registration}
\end{equation}
ICP solves the same problem when the pairs are unknown.

\emph{Pose graph.} The nodes are the poses $\x_0,\dots,\x_{K-1}$, and
two kinds of edges connect them. Odometry edges follow the motion model:
the measured motion $\mathbf{u}_k=(\mathbf{u}^{xy}_k,u^\theta_k)$ from scan
$k-1$ to scan $k$, in the frame of $k-1$, predicts the next pose,
\begin{equation}
\x_k=f(\x_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_k)+\mathbf{w}_k,\quad
f(\x,\mathbf{u})=\begin{bmatrix}\tb+R(\theta)\,\mathbf{u}^{xy}\\ \theta+u^{\theta}\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:pgmotion}
\end{equation}
Loop-closure edges are observations: when scans $i$ and $j$ see the same
walls, ICP between them measures $\mathbf{z}_{ij}$, the pose of $j$ seen
from $i$, with observation model
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{z}_{ij}=h(\x_i,\x_j)+\mathbf{v}_{ij},\quad
h(\x_i,\x_j)=\begin{bmatrix}R(\theta_i)^\top(\tb_j-\tb_i)\\ \theta_j-\theta_i\end{bmatrix},
\label{eq:pgobs}
\end{equation}
which is $T_i^{-1}T_j$ written as numbers. With Gaussian noise
$\mathbf{w}_k\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_{\text{odo}})$,
$\mathbf{v}_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\Sigma_{\text{loop}})$ and
information matrices $\Omega=\Sigma^{-1}$, the most probable path minimizes
the weighted squared residuals (angles wrapped to $[-\pi,\pi)$):
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf{e}_k=\x_k-f(\x_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_k),\qquad
\mathbf{e}_{ij}=\mathbf{z}_{ij}-h(\x_i,\x_j),\\
\mathcal{J}(\x)=\tfrac12\sum_k\mathbf{e}_k^\top\Omega_{\text{odo}}\mathbf{e}_k
+\tfrac12\sum_{(i,j)}\mathbf{e}_{ij}^\top\Omega_{\text{loop}}\mathbf{e}_{ij}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:posegraph}
\end{equation}
This is the lecture's Graph SLAM with the landmark term
$\mathbf{z}-h(\x,\mathbf{m})$ replaced by $\mathbf{z}_{ij}-h(\x_i,\x_j)$: the
map is not in the state but is built afterwards from the optimized poses.
GTSAM stores both edge types as relative-pose factors. Since
$\sigma_x=\sigma_y$, the two residual forms give the same cost (in $x,y$,
$\x_k-f=-R(\theta_{k-1})(\mathbf{u}_k-h)$), and GTSAM's own error agrees for
small angle errors (2.09836 vs.\ 2.09835 at 0.01\,rad).

\emph{Occupancy grid.} Each cell $m_i$ holds the log-odds
$\lambda_i=\log\frac{p(m_i=1\mid z_{1:t})}{p(m_i=0\mid z_{1:t})}$, with
$\lambda_i=0$ meaning unknown (50\,\%).

\subsection{Odometry}

\summary{Finds a first path from the wheels and the gyro. Input: encoder
counts and IMU yaw rate. Returns one pose $(x,y,\theta)$ per encoder reading
(4956 for dataset 20). Errors add up step by step, so the path drifts.}

\step{1} Distance per tick, wheel diameter $D=0.254$\,m, 360 ticks per turn:
$s=\pi D/360\approx0.0022$\,m.

\step{2} Side travel from the counts $FR,FL,RR,RL$ since the last reading:
\begin{equation}
d_R=\tfrac{FR+RR}{2}\,s,\qquad d_L=\tfrac{FL+RL}{2}\,s.
\end{equation}

\step{3} The center of a differential-drive robot moves by the average of
the two sides, so with $\tau$ the encoder time step,
\begin{equation}
l=\tfrac{d_R+d_L}{2},\qquad v=\tfrac{l}{\tau}.
\end{equation}

\step{4} Yaw rate: the wheels give $\omega=(d_R-d_L)/(b\,\tau)$ with track
width $b$, but wheels slip in turns, so $\omega$ is taken from the IMU
$z$-axis gyro at the nearest IMU time stamp.

\step{5} Integrate with the Euler step~\eqref{eq:motion}, assuming $v$ and
$\omega$ constant over each $\tau$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Odometry}\label{alg:odometry}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require counts $FR,FL,RR,RL$, stamps, IMU yaw rate
\Ensure $\x_t=(x_t,y_t,\theta_t)$ for $t=0,\dots,N-1$
\State $\x_0\gets(0,0,0)$
\For{$t=1,\dots,N-1$}
  \State $\tau\gets$ stamp$_t-$stamp$_{t-1}$
  \State $d_R\gets(FR_t+RR_t)\,s/2$,\ \ $d_L\gets(FL_t+RL_t)\,s/2$
  \State $v\gets(d_R+d_L)/(2\tau)$,\ \ $\omega\gets$ IMU yaw rate nearest in time
  \State $\theta_t\gets\theta_{t-1}+\omega\tau$
  \State $(x_t,y_t)\gets(x_{t-1},y_{t-1})+v\tau(\cos\theta_t,\sin\theta_t)$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Kabsch algorithm (known pairs)}

\summary{Finds the rotation and translation that best move point set $P$
onto $Q$ when row $k$ of $P$ belongs with row $k$ of $Q$. Returns $R$,
$\tb$ and the RMSD (root mean square distance) of the aligned points. No
iteration: one SVD gives the exact answer.}

\step{1} Objective~\eqref{eq:registration}:
$E(R,\tb)=\sum_k\lVert\q_k-R\p_k-\tb\rVert^2$.

\step{2} Optimal translation. Setting the gradient to zero,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial E}{\partial\tb}=-2\sum_k(\q_k-R\p_k-\tb)=0
\;\Rightarrow\;
\tb^*=\bar{\q}-R\bar{\p},
\end{equation}
with centroids $\bar{\p}=\frac1N\sum_k\p_k$, $\bar{\q}=\frac1N\sum_k\q_k$.
This is why centroids are used: the best translation always moves the
source centroid onto the target centroid, so after centering both sets the
translation drops out and the rotation can be solved alone.

\step{3} Centered points $\p'_k=\p_k-\bar{\p}$, $\q'_k=\q_k-\bar{\q}$.
Substituting $\tb^*$ and expanding, with $\lVert R\p'_k\rVert=\lVert\p'_k\rVert$:
\begin{equation}
E(R)=\sum_k\big(\lVert\q'_k\rVert^2+\lVert\p'_k\rVert^2\big)-2\sum_k\q'^\top_kR\,\p'_k .
\end{equation}
The first sum does not depend on $R$, so minimizing $E$ means maximizing the
last sum.

\step{4} Trace form. Since $\q'^\top R\p'=\operatorname{tr}(R\,\p'\q'^\top)$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\textstyle\sum_k\q'^\top_kR\,\p'_k&=\operatorname{tr}(RH),\\
H&=\textstyle\sum_k\p'_k\q'^\top_k=P'^\top Q'\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\step{5} SVD. Every real matrix factors as
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
H=U\Sigma V^\top,\quad U^\top U=V^\top V=I,\\
\Sigma=\operatorname{diag}(\sigma_1\ge\dots\ge\sigma_d\ge0),
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ a rotation (or reflection), a scaling along the axes, and another
rotation (or reflection). $\sigma_i$ are the singular values.

\step{6} Bound. By the cyclic property of the trace,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{tr}(RH)&=\operatorname{tr}(RU\Sigma V^\top)=\operatorname{tr}(\Sigma\,V^\top RU)\\
&=\textstyle\sum_i\sigma_iM_{ii},\qquad M=V^\top RU.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$M$ is orthogonal, so $|M_{ii}|\le1$ and $\operatorname{tr}(RH)\le\sum_i\sigma_i$.
Equality holds for $M=I$, so $R^*=VU^\top$.

\step{7} Reflection fix. If $\det(VU^\top)=-1$, $VU^\top$ is a mirror, not a
rotation. The best proper rotation flips the axis with the smallest
$\sigma_d$:
\begin{equation}
R^*=V\operatorname{diag}\!\big(1,\dots,1,\det(VU^\top)\big)U^\top,
\end{equation}
which the code does by negating the last row of $V^\top$. A self-test
rotates a random cloud by $40^\circ$ and recovers $R$ and $\tb$ exactly.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Kabsch}\label{alg:kabsch}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}$, row $k$ paired with row $k$
\Ensure $R$, $\tb$, RMSD
\State $\bar\p,\bar\q\gets$ means of the rows;\ \ $P'\gets P-\bar\p$,\ \ $Q'\gets Q-\bar\q$
\State $H\gets P'^\top Q'$ \Comment{$d\times d$}
\State $U,\Sigma,V^\top\gets\operatorname{SVD}(H)$
\State $R\gets V\operatorname{diag}(1,\dots,1,\det(VU^\top))\,U^\top$
\State $\tb\gets\bar\q-R\bar\p$
\State \Return $R$, $\tb$, $\sqrt{\tfrac1N\sum_k\lVert R\p'_k-\q'_k\rVert^2}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{ICP (unknown pairs)}

\summary{Finds the transform $T$ that moves a source point set onto a target
point set when the pairs are unknown, by alternating two steps: guess the
pairs (nearest neighbors), then solve Kabsch on them. Input: source, target,
initial guess $T^0$. Returns the final $T$ and the mean pair distance.}

\step{1} Start from $T^0$ and build a KD-tree on the target once.

\step{2} Transform: $\tilde\p_k=R^n\p_k+\tb^n$.

\step{3} Correspondence (nearest neighbor):
\begin{equation*}
j(k)=\arg\min_j\lVert\q_j-\tilde\p_k\rVert .
\end{equation*}

\step{4} Outlier rejection:
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal{I}=\{k:\lVert\q_{j(k)}-\tilde\p_k\rVert<d_{\max}\}.
\end{equation*}

\step{5} Update with Kabsch on the kept pairs, using the original $\p_k$, so
the result is the full transform, not an increment:
\begin{equation*}
(R^{n+1},\tb^{n+1})=\text{Kabsch}\big(\{\p_k,\q_{j(k)}\}_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\big).
\end{equation*}

\step{6} Error and stopping rule:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
e^n=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{I}|}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\lVert\q_{j(k)}-\tilde\p_k\rVert,\\
\text{stop if }|e^{n-1}-e^n|<10^{-6}\ \text{or}\ n=50.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}

\emph{Convergence.} With $E(T,j)$ the squared error~\eqref{eq:registration} for transform $T$ and pairing $j$,
\begin{equation}
E(T^n,j^n)\ \ge\ E(T^n,j^{n+1})\ \ge\ E(T^{n+1},j^{n+1}),
\end{equation}
because step 3 picks the closest pairs for the current $T$ and step 5 picks
the best $T$ for those pairs. A non-increasing sequence bounded below by 0
converges, so ICP stops, but only at the nearest local minimum. A far-off
$T^0$ gives wrong pairs from the first iteration, so a good initial guess is
required. (With $d_{\max}$ the kept set changes, so the decrease is nearly,
not strictly, monotone.)

\emph{Warm-up.} The partial view is registered onto the full model and the
result inverted: every view point has a partner on the model, but the
hidden back of the model has none in the view. ICP is started from 12 yaws
($0^\circ,30^\circ,\dots,330^\circ$), each with the centroids aligned, using
every 10th view point; the start with the lowest error is refined with all
view points.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{ICP}\label{alg:icp}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require source $\{\p_k\}$, target $\{\q_j\}$, $T^0$, $d_{\max}$
\Ensure $T$ (source $\to$ target), mean pair distance $e$
\State build a KD-tree of the target;\ \ $e^{-1}\gets\infty$
\For{$n=0,\dots,49$}
  \State $\tilde\p_k\gets R^n\p_k+\tb^n$ for all $k$
  \State $j(k)\gets$ nearest target point to $\tilde\p_k$ \Comment{new pairs}
  \State $\mathcal{I}\gets\{k:\lVert\q_{j(k)}-\tilde\p_k\rVert<d_{\max}\}$
  \State $e^n\gets$ mean of $\lVert\q_{j(k)}-\tilde\p_k\rVert$ over $\mathcal{I}$
  \State $(R^{n+1},\tb^{n+1})\gets\text{Kabsch}(\{\p_k\},\{\q_{j(k)}\})_{k\in\mathcal{I}}$ \Comment{new $T$}
  \State \textbf{if} $|e^{n-1}-e^n|<10^{-6}$ \textbf{then break}
\EndFor
\State \Return $T^{n+1}$, $e^n$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{LiDAR scan matching}

\summary{Finds the pose of every LiDAR scan by matching it with ICP against a
keyframe scan, starting from the odometry motion. Returns $K$ poses (4962
for dataset 20). Less drift than odometry, but still some.}

\step{1} Ranges to body points: for beam $n$ with range $r_n$,
$\alpha_n=\alpha_{\min}+n\Delta\alpha$ and
\begin{equation}
\p_n=\big(r_n\cos\alpha_n+0.133,\ r_n\sin\alpha_n\big),\quad r_{\min}<r_n<r_{\max},
\end{equation}
where $0.133$\,m (0.13323 in the code) is the LiDAR offset from the robot
center~\cite{config}. $S_k=\{\p_n\}$ is the point set of scan $k$.

\step{2} Relative pose. A wall point has one world position:
$T_i\p^{(i)}=T_j\p^{(j)}\Rightarrow\p^{(i)}=(T_i^{-1}T_j)\,\p^{(j)}$. So ICP of
scan $j$ onto scan $i$ estimates $T_i^{-1}T_j$.

\step{3} Initial guess from odometry poses $O$, relative to keyframe $\text{kf}$:
\begin{equation}
T^0=T_{\text{kf}}^{-1}\,T_{k-1}\,(O_{k-1}^{-1}O_k).
\end{equation}
$O_{k-1}^{-1}O_k$ is the odometry step in the robot frame, so it can be
stacked on the ICP pose $T_{k-1}$ even after the two paths drift apart.

\step{4} Match and compose:
$T_{\text{rel}}=\text{ICP}(S_k,S_{\text{kf}},T^0)$ with $d_{\max}=0.5$\,m, then $T_k=T_{\text{kf}}T_{\text{rel}}$.

\step{5} Scan $k$ becomes the keyframe when $\lVert\tb_{\text{rel}}\rVert>0.5$\,m
or $|\theta_{\text{rel}}|>0.2$\,rad. Consecutive scans are only 2\,cm apart;
ICP under-reports such a small motion in a hallway, and chaining 4962 such
steps made the hallways too short.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Scan matching}\label{alg:scanmatching}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require scans $S_k$, odometry poses $O_k$ (nearest in time)
\Ensure ICP poses $T_0,\dots,T_{K-1}$
\State $T_0\gets O_0$;\ \ $\text{kf}\gets0$
\For{$k=1,\dots,K-1$}
  \State $T^0\gets T_{\text{kf}}^{-1}T_{k-1}O_{k-1}^{-1}O_k$ \Comment{initial guess}
  \State $T_{\text{rel}}\gets\text{ICP}(S_k,S_{\text{kf}},T^0,d_{\max}{=}0.5)$
  \State $T_k\gets T_{\text{kf}}T_{\text{rel}}$
  \State \textbf{if} $\lVert\tb_{\text{rel}}\rVert>0.5$ or $|\theta_{\text{rel}}|>0.2$ \textbf{then} $\text{kf}\gets k$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Pose graph optimization}

\summary{Finds the path that best agrees with all measurements at once.
Input: ICP poses and scans. Returns $K$ optimized poses. Iterative: each
Levenberg-Marquardt step moves all poses a little.}

\emph{Idea.} Every pose is a node and every measurement between two poses
is an edge, like a spring that says ``pose $j$ should sit here as seen from
pose $i$''. Odometry edges link consecutive poses; loop-closure edges link
two visits to the same place. The optimizer moves all poses at once until
the springs are as relaxed as possible; a smaller $\sigma$ is a stiffer
spring.

\step{1} Nodes: $\x=(\x_0,\dots,\x_{K-1})$, $3K=14886$ unknowns, starting
at the ICP poses. The map is not in the state; it is drawn afterwards from
the final poses.

\step{2} Odometry edges. $\mathbf{u}_k=T_{k-1}^{-1}T_k$ is the ICP step from
scan $k-1$ to scan $k$, in the frame of $k-1$. Composing the two matrices
gives the motion model~\eqref{eq:pgmotion}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
T_{k-1}U_k=\begin{bmatrix}R_{k-1}R(u^\theta_k)&\tb_{k-1}+R_{k-1}\mathbf{u}^{xy}_k\\ \mathbf{0}^\top&1\end{bmatrix}\\
\Rightarrow\ f(\x_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_k)=\begin{bmatrix}\tb_{k-1}+R_{k-1}\mathbf{u}^{xy}_k\\ \theta_{k-1}+u^\theta_k\end{bmatrix},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with residual $\mathbf{e}_k=\x_k-f(\x_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_k)$ and
$\sigma=(0.01,0.01,0.005)$.

\step{3} Loop-closure edges. When the robot comes back to a place, ICP
between the old scan $i$ and the new scan $j$ measures $\mathbf{z}_{ij}$,
the pose of $j$ seen from $i$, directly from the walls, so it does not
contain the drift built up in between. The prediction is the relative
pose~\eqref{eq:pgobs}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
T_i^{-1}T_j=\begin{bmatrix}R_i^\top R_j&R_i^\top(\tb_j-\tb_i)\\ \mathbf{0}^\top&1\end{bmatrix}\\
\Rightarrow\ h(\x_i,\x_j)=\begin{bmatrix}R_i^\top(\tb_j-\tb_i)\\ \theta_j-\theta_i\end{bmatrix},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
with residual $\mathbf{e}_{ij}=\mathbf{z}_{ij}-h(\x_i,\x_j)$. Pairs are found
in two ways: fixed-interval pairs $(i,i+10)$, with ICP $d_{\max}=0.5$\,m;
and proximity pairs among every 20th scan, closer than 2\,m in the ICP path
but at least 500 scans apart in time, with $d_{\max}=1.0$\,m. ICP starts
from $T_i^{-1}T_j$. ICP returns an answer even for scans that do not
overlap, so a match is kept only if most of scan $j$ lands on scan $i$:
\begin{equation}
\rho_{ij}=\frac{\#\{\p\in S_j:\ d(Z_{ij}\p,\,S_i)<0.1\,\text{m}\}}{|S_j|}\ \ge\ 0.6,
\end{equation}
where $Z_{ij}$ moves scan $j$ by the ICP result and $d(\q,S_i)$ is the
distance from $\q$ to the nearest point of $S_i$. Loop edges use
$\sigma=(0.1,0.1,0.05)$, ten times looser than odometry.

\step{4} Prior: $\mathbf{e}_0=\x_0-\bar{\x}_0$ ($\bar{\x}_0$ the first ICP
pose), $\sigma=10^{-3}$. Step 10 explains why it is needed.

\step{5} Cost from probability. The estimate is the most probable path,
$\arg\max_{\x}p(\x\mid\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z})$. If each measurement depends
only on the two poses it links and the noises are independent,
\begin{equation}
p(\x\mid\mathbf{u},\mathbf{z})\propto p(\x_0)\prod_k p(\x_k\mid\x_{k-1},\mathbf{u}_k)\prod_{(i,j)}p(\mathbf{z}_{ij}\mid\x_i,\x_j).
\end{equation}
Each factor is Gaussian, $\propto\exp(-\tfrac12\mathbf{e}^\top\Omega\,\mathbf{e})$
with $\Omega=\Sigma^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(1/\sigma_x^2,1/\sigma_y^2,1/\sigma_\theta^2)$,
so $-\log$ turns the product into the sum $\mathcal{J}$
of~\eqref{eq:posegraph} plus $\tfrac12\mathbf{e}_0^\top\Omega_0\mathbf{e}_0$:
the most probable path is the weighted least-squares path. Since the
$\mathbf{u}_k$ come from the ICP poses, without loop closures the ICP path
makes every residual zero. Only the loop closures change the path; the
optimizer spreads their disagreement over all edges.

\step{6} Robust loop edges. Let
$\varepsilon=\sqrt{\mathbf{e}^\top\Omega\,\mathbf{e}}$ be the size of a
residual in sigmas. In least squares an edge costs $\tfrac12\varepsilon^2$
and pulls with force $\varepsilon$ (the slope), so a wrong loop closure
$10\sigma$ off pulls with 10 and can bend the map. Loop edges therefore use
the Huber cost, built from the pull: keep the least-squares pull for small
errors, cap it at 1 for large ones, and integrate the pull to get the cost:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\psi(\varepsilon)&=\rho_H'(\varepsilon)=\min(\varepsilon,1),\\
\varepsilon\le1:\ \rho_H(\varepsilon)&=\textstyle\int_0^{\varepsilon}s\,ds=\tfrac12\varepsilon^2,\\
\varepsilon>1:\ \rho_H(\varepsilon)&=\textstyle\int_0^{1}s\,ds+\int_1^{\varepsilon}1\,ds=\varepsilon-\tfrac12.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Both pieces give $\tfrac12$ with slope 1 at $\varepsilon=1$, so the cost has
no jump or kink. As a probability, $\exp(-\rho_H)$ has heavier tails than a
Gaussian: a large error is rare but possible, so a bad match is treated as
a mistake and not explained by bending the map. Gauss-Newton needs squares,
so each iteration writes $\rho_H\approx\tfrac12w\varepsilon^2$ with
$w=\psi(\varepsilon)/\varepsilon=\min(1,1/\varepsilon)$, i.e.\ the loop
edge's $\Omega$ is scaled by $w$ ($w=0.1$ for an edge $10\sigma$ off).

\step{7} Linearize. $\mathcal{J}$ is not quadratic in $\x$ because $R(\theta)$
contains $\cos$ and $\sin$, so it is minimized by iterating: near the
current guess $\x^{(n)}$, each residual is replaced by a straight line.
A residual depends on two poses $a,b$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf{e}(\x^{(n)}+\delta\x)\approx\mathbf{e}+A\,\delta\x_a+B\,\delta\x_b,\\
A=\frac{\partial\mathbf{e}}{\partial\x_a},\qquad B=\frac{\partial\mathbf{e}}{\partial\x_b},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $A$ says how the residual changes when pose $a$ moves a little, and
$B$ the same for pose $b$. The derivative of a rotation is
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\frac{dR}{d\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\theta&-\cos\theta\\ \cos\theta&-\sin\theta\end{bmatrix}=R\,S,\quad
S=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix},\\
\frac{dR^\top}{d\theta}=(RS)^\top=-S R^\top.
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Odometry: $\mathbf{e}^{xy}_k=\tb_k-\tb_{k-1}-R_{k-1}\mathbf{u}^{xy}_k$ and
$e^\theta_k=\theta_k-\theta_{k-1}-u^\theta_k$, so entry by entry
\begin{equation}
A=\begin{bmatrix}-I_2&-R_{k-1}S\,\mathbf{u}^{xy}_k\\ \mathbf{0}^\top&-1\end{bmatrix},\qquad B=I_3.
\end{equation}
Loop: $\mathbf{e}^{xy}_{ij}=\mathbf{z}^{xy}_{ij}-R_i^\top(\tb_j-\tb_i)$ and
$e^\theta_{ij}=z^\theta_{ij}-\theta_j+\theta_i$, so
\begin{equation}
A=\begin{bmatrix}R_i^\top&S R_i^\top(\tb_j-\tb_i)\\ \mathbf{0}^\top&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad
B=\begin{bmatrix}-R_i^\top&\mathbf{0}\\ \mathbf{0}^\top&-1\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}

\step{8} Normal equations. With the straight-line residuals, each edge's
cost becomes a quadratic in $\delta\x$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&\tfrac12(\mathbf{e}+J\delta\x)^\top\Omega(\mathbf{e}+J\delta\x)\\
&=\tfrac12\mathbf{e}^\top\Omega\mathbf{e}+\delta\x^\top J^\top\Omega\mathbf{e}+\tfrac12\delta\x^\top J^\top\Omega J\,\delta\x,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $J$ is the edge's row of blocks: $A$ at pose $a$, $B$ at pose $b$,
zero elsewhere. Summing over edges,
$\mathcal{J}\approx\text{const}+\delta\x^\top\mathbf{b}+\tfrac12\delta\x^\top H\delta\x$
with $H=\sum J^\top\Omega J$ and $\mathbf{b}=\sum J^\top\Omega\,\mathbf{e}$.
Setting the gradient $\mathbf{b}+H\delta\x$ to zero gives
$H\,\delta\x=-\mathbf{b}$. $H$ is a table of $3\times3$ blocks, one per pair
of poses. An edge involves only poses $a$ and $b$, so it adds to only four
blocks of $H$ and two of $\mathbf{b}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
H_{aa}&\mathrel{+}=A^\top\Omega A, & H_{ab}&\mathrel{+}=A^\top\Omega B,\\
H_{ba}&\mathrel{+}=B^\top\Omega A, & H_{bb}&\mathrel{+}=B^\top\Omega B,\\
\mathbf{b}_a&\mathrel{+}=A^\top\Omega\,\mathbf{e}, & \mathbf{b}_b&\mathrel{+}=B^\top\Omega\,\mathbf{e}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The prior adds $\Omega_0$ to $H_{00}$ and $\Omega_0\mathbf{e}_0$ to $\mathbf{b}_0$.

\step{9} Update and iterate (Levenberg-Marquardt):
\begin{equation}
(H+\mu I)\,\delta\x=-\mathbf{b},\qquad \x^{(n+1)}=\x^{(n)}+\delta\x
\end{equation}
(angles wrapped). In every iteration $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{z}$ and $\Omega$
stay fixed, while $\mathbf{e}$, $A$, $B$, $H$ and $\mathbf{b}$ are
recomputed at $\x^{(n)}$. With $\mu\to0$ the step is Gauss-Newton,
$-H^{-1}\mathbf{b}$; with large $\mu$ it is $-\mathbf{b}/\mu$, a short step
downhill, since $\mathbf{b}$ is the gradient of $\mathcal{J}$. If
$\mathcal{J}$ dropped, the step is kept and $\mu$ decreases; otherwise it is
rejected and $\mu$ increases. GTSAM stops when $\mathcal{J}$ decreases by
less than $10^{-5}$ (relative or absolute), or after 100 iterations.

\step{10} Prior and sparsity. Shifting every pose by the same $\mathbf{a}$,
$\delta\x_a=\delta\x_b=(\mathbf{a},0)$, gives
$A\delta\x_a+B\delta\x_b=-\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0}$ for odometry and
$R_i^\top\mathbf{a}-R_i^\top\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{0}$ for loops, so
$H\delta\x=\mathbf{0}$: relative measurements say nothing about where the
whole map sits, and $H$ is singular. The prior adds $\Omega_0=10^6I$ to
$H_{00}$, which removes this. Each edge touches 2 poses, so only
$4962+2\cdot5578$ of the $4962^2$ blocks of $H$ are non-zero (about
0.07\,\%), and sparse elimination solves it fast.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Pose graph optimization}\label{alg:posegraph}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require ICP poses $T_k$, scans $S_k$
\Ensure optimized poses $\x^*$
\State add prior on $\x_0$; add odometry edges $\mathbf{u}_k=T_{k-1}^{-1}T_k$
\For{each candidate pair $(i,j)$}
  \State $\mathbf{z}_{ij}\gets\text{ICP}(S_j,S_i,T_i^{-1}T_j)$
  \State \textbf{if} $\rho_{ij}\ge0.6$ \textbf{then} add loop edge $(i,j,\mathbf{z}_{ij})$, Huber
\EndFor
\State $\x^{(0)}\gets$ ICP poses
\Repeat \Comment{$n=0,1,\dots$}
  \State $\mathbf{e},A,B$ of every edge at $\x^{(n)}$; build $H$, $\mathbf{b}$
  \State solve $(H+\mu I)\,\delta\x=-\mathbf{b}$
  \If{$\mathcal{J}(\x^{(n)}+\delta\x)<\mathcal{J}(\x^{(n)})$}
    \State $\x^{(n+1)}\gets\x^{(n)}+\delta\x$;\ \ decrease $\mu$
  \Else
    \State $\x^{(n+1)}\gets\x^{(n)}$;\ \ increase $\mu$
  \EndIf
\Until{$\mathcal{J}$ decreases by less than $10^{-5}$, or 100 iterations}
\State \Return $\x^{(n)}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\emph{Example.} Three poses on a line, all $\Omega=I$, the prior holding
$x_0=0$. Odometry says $0\to1$ and $1\to2$ are each 1\,m; a loop closure
says $z_{02}=1.8$\,m. The residuals are $e_{01}=x_1-x_0-1$,
$e_{12}=x_2-x_1-1$ and $e_{02}=1.8-(x_2-x_0)$. Iteration 1, at $x_1=1$,
$x_2=2$: residuals $(0,0,-0.2)$, $\mathcal{J}=0.02$. Their derivatives with
respect to $(x_1,x_2)$ are $(1,0)$, $(-1,1)$ and $(0,-1)$, so
$H=\sum J^\top J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
$\mathbf{b}=\sum J^\top e=(0,0.2)$ and
$\delta\x=-H^{-1}\mathbf{b}=(-0.067,-0.133)$. Iteration 2, at $x_1=0.933$,
$x_2=1.867$: residuals $(-0.067,-0.067,-0.067)$, $\mathcal{J}=0.0067$,
$\mathbf{b}=0$, so it stops. The 0.2\,m disagreement is shared equally; with
unequal $\Omega$, the stiffer edges keep smaller residuals.

Checks: for one test factor, $\tfrac12\sum(e/\sigma)^2=10.5001$ by hand and
from \code{factor.error()}; steps 7 to 9 coded by hand on a 5-pose test loop
reach GTSAM's optimum to $3\times10^{-8}$, and both Jacobians match finite
differences. The small odometry $\sigma$ is deliberate: with $\sigma=5$\,cm
the loop closures shrank the path to 86.5\,m (Table~\ref{tab:tuning}). A
likely reason: in a straight corridor, sliding one scan along the other
barely changes the overlap, so loop matches are weak along the corridor,
and loose odometry edges let them pull the hallways shorter.

\subsection{Occupancy grid mapping}

\summary{Finds which grid cells contain walls. Input: optimized poses and
scans. Returns a $1600\times1600$ grid of log-odds (positive = wall, negative
= free, 0 = never seen). Each beam lowers the cells it passes through and
raises the cell where it ends.}

\step{1} Beams in the world: $\p_W=R_k\p+\tb_k$ and LiDAR origin
$\mathbf{o}=T_k[0.133,0,1]^\top$.

\step{2} Cell index with resolution $r=0.05$\,m and map corner $\mathbf{m}_{\min}=(-30,-30)$\,m:
$\operatorname{cell}(\p)=\lfloor(\p-\mathbf{m}_{\min})/r\rfloor$, on a $1600\times1600$ grid.

\step{3} Cells on each beam: $\{c_1,\dots,c_L\}=\text{Bresenham}\big(\operatorname{cell}(\mathbf{o}),\operatorname{cell}(\p_W)\big)$.

\step{4} Bayes' rule for one cell:
$p(m\mid z_{1:t})=\eta\,p(z_t\mid m)\,p(m\mid z_{1:t-1})$, with normalizer $\eta=1/p(z_t\mid z_{1:t-1})$.

\step{5} Divide the expression for $m=1$ by the one for $m=0$; $\eta$ cancels:
\begin{equation}
\frac{p(m{=}1\mid z_{1:t})}{p(m{=}0\mid z_{1:t})}
=g\cdot\frac{p(m{=}1\mid z_{1:t-1})}{p(m{=}0\mid z_{1:t-1})},\quad
g=\frac{p(z_t\mid m{=}1)}{p(z_t\mid m{=}0)}.
\end{equation}

\step{6} Take the log, so the product becomes a sum:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_t=\lambda_{t-1}+\log g .
\end{equation}

\step{7} Sensor model with 80\,\% trust ($g=0.8/0.2=4$): the beam passed
through $c_1,\dots,c_{L-1}$, so $\lambda\mathrel{-}=\log4$ (free), and it ended
in $c_L$, so $\lambda\mathrel{+}=\log4$ (occupied). Clip to $[-20,20]$ so cells
can still change. Every 2nd scan is used.

\step{8} Probability: $p(m{=}1)=1-\dfrac{1}{1+e^{\lambda}}$ (the logistic function).

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Occupancy grid}\label{alg:occupancy}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require poses $T_k$, scans $S_k$
\Ensure log-odds grid $\lambda$
\State $\lambda\gets0$ ($1600\times1600$)
\For{$k=0,2,4,\dots$} \Comment{every 2nd scan}
  \State $\mathbf{o}\gets\operatorname{cell}(T_k[0.133,0,1]^\top)$
  \For{each $\p\in S_k$}
    \State $c_1,\dots,c_L\gets\text{Bresenham}(\mathbf{o},\operatorname{cell}(R_k\p+\tb_k))$
    \State $\lambda(c_1..c_{L-1})\mathrel{-}=\log4$;\ \ $\lambda(c_L)\mathrel{+}=\log4$
  \EndFor
  \State clip $\lambda$ to $[-20,20]$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Floor texture mapping}

\summary{Finds the floor color of each grid cell. The occupancy grid only
says where walls are; the texture map adds what the floor looks like, and
since it comes from a second sensor it also checks the poses by eye: a wrong
pose paints the same floor patch in two places. Input: optimized poses,
Kinect disparity and RGB images. Returns a $1600\times1600\times3$ color image
on the same grid as the occupancy map. Each depth pixel becomes a 3-D
point; floor points are moved to the world and painted with their color.}

For every 5th Kinect image, with the nearest RGB image and pose in time:

\step{1} Depth from disparity $d$ (handout calibration):
$dd=-0.00304\,d+3.31$, $z=1.03/dd$.

\step{2} Matching RGB pixel for depth pixel $(u,v)$ (column, row):
\begin{equation}
u_{\text{rgb}}=\tfrac{526.37\,u+16662}{585.051},\quad
v_{\text{rgb}}=\tfrac{526.37\,v+19276-7877.07\,dd}{585.051}.
\end{equation}

\step{3} Pinhole model. Projection with intrinsics $K$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
z\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\1\end{bmatrix}=K\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{bmatrix},\quad
K=\begin{bmatrix}f&0&c_u\\0&f&c_v\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix}\\
\Rightarrow\
\begin{bmatrix}x\\y\\z\end{bmatrix}=z\,K^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}u\\v\\1\end{bmatrix},
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
so $x=(u-c_u)z/f$, $y=(v-c_v)z/f$, with $f=585.05$, $c_u=315.84$,
$c_v=242.94$ (optical frame: $x$ right, $y$ down, $z$ forward).

\step{4} Optical to camera frame ($x$ forward, $y$ left, $z$ up):
$\p_C=(z,-x,-y)$.

\step{5} Camera to body: $\p_B=R_z(\psi)R_y(\phi)R_x(\gamma)\,\p_C+[0.18,0.005,0.36]^\top$,
with roll $\gamma=0$, pitch $\phi=0.36$, yaw $\psi=0.021$\,rad. Each factor keeps its own axis
fixed and rotates the other two, e.g.
\begin{equation}
R_y(\phi)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi&0&\sin\phi\\0&1&0\\-\sin\phi&0&\cos\phi\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The camera forward axis maps to $R[1,0,0]^\top=(0.936,0.020,-0.352)$, i.e.\
$20.6^\circ$ below horizontal.

\step{6} Floor filter: keep $0<z<5$\,m and $|p_{B,z}-z_{\text{floor}}|<0.05$\,m,
with $z_{\text{floor}}=-0.14$\,m measured from the data (not 0), and the RGB pixel inside the image.

\step{7} World and paint: $\p_W=R_k\,\p_{B,xy}+\tb_k$, then
$c\big(\operatorname{cell}(\p_W)\big)\leftarrow\text{RGB}(u_{\text{rgb}},v_{\text{rgb}})$.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Floor texture}\label{alg:texture}
\footnotesize
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require poses, disparity and RGB images
\Ensure color map $c$ ($1600\times1600\times3$)
\State $c\gets$ black
\For{every 5th disparity image} \Comment{all pixels at once}
  \State RGB image and pose $T_k$ nearest in time
  \State $dd,z\gets$ depth (step 1);\ \ $u_{\text{rgb}},v_{\text{rgb}}$ (step 2)
  \State $\p_C\gets(z,-(u-c_u)z/f,-(v-c_v)z/f)$ \Comment{steps 3, 4}
  \State $\p_B\gets R_z(\psi)R_y(\phi)R_x(\gamma)\p_C+[0.18,0.005,0.36]^\top$
  \State keep floor pixels (step 6)
  \State $c(\operatorname{cell}(R_k\p_{B,xy}+\tb_k))\gets\text{RGB}(u_{\text{rgb}},v_{\text{rgb}})$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

ICP lowers the cross-pass error from 18.7 to 10.8\,cm (dataset 20) and
from 17.1 to 11.4\,cm (dataset 21), but still ends 2.7 to 2.8\,m from the
start (Table~\ref{tab:results}, Fig.~\ref{fig:trajectory}). The pose graph closes
the loop: 0.12 and 0.22\,m, with 2.7 and 1.1\,cm cross-pass error. It
accepted 496 fixed-interval and 121 of 875 proximity closures on dataset 20,
and 475 and 233 of 832 on dataset 21. On dataset 20, 758 of the 875
candidates pair passes in opposite directions, and only 11\,\% of those
pass the inlier test, against 33\,\% of same-direction pairs; facing the
other way, the 270$^\circ$ LiDAR likely sees different walls.

Fixed-interval closures alone change almost nothing, since scans 10 apart
repeat what the odometry factors already state; the proximity closures
remove the drift (Table~\ref{tab:tuning}). Loose odometry noise gives the
best cross-pass value but shortens the path by 9\,m, so path length is
checked as well.

\emph{Maps.} Figs.~\ref{fig:maps20} and~\ref{fig:maps21} show the maps over
time and ICP vs.\ optimized poses at the end. With ICP poses the return pass
is about 11\,cm off and the path end misses the start, which blurs the start
area and the walls. With optimized poses both passes overlap, and the
path forms an L about 23\,m by 22\,m. Run time: scan matching
43\,s, pose graph 30\,s, occupancy map 70\,s, texture map 20\,s.



\emph{Discussion.} Odometry drifts from wheel slip and gyro bias; ICP uses
the walls but only compares nearby scans, so it still drifts; the pose
graph links scans far apart in time, which removes the drift. Three early
failures and their fixes: matching to the previous scan shrank the hallways
(keyframes), loose odometry noise let loop closures shrink the path (small $\sigma$), and ICP from
a bad start locked onto a wrong pose (12 initial yaws). Cross-pass
consistency only checks that repeated passes agree, not the overall shape:
on dataset 20 the two hallways meet at $91^\circ$ with ICP poses and at
$82^\circ$ with optimized poses (directions fitted to the path). If the real
hallways are perpendicular, closing the loop bent the corner by about
$9^\circ$; ground truth would be needed to confirm this.
